# 시청자에게 "남의 업무 고통"을 느끼게 하는 법 — 심리학·커뮤니케이션 연구 정리
### For the 마인드스코프(MOEUM) B2B product video

**Verification note:** every citation below was checked against the publisher/PubMed/repository record via search. I read abstracts and published summaries, not full texts, except where noted. Items I could **not** verify are flagged explicitly in §7. Effect sizes quoted are as reported in the source records.

---

## 1. 스토리 안으로 들여보내기 — Transportation & Identification

**1. Green & Brock (2000).** *The role of transportation in the persuasiveness of public narratives.* JPSP, 79(5), 701–721. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11079236/
Transportation = 몰입(imagery + affect + attentional focus). 4 experiments (N=97/69/274/258): higher transportation → story-consistent beliefs and more favorable protagonist evaluation. Critically, **Exp. 2: transported readers found fewer "false notes"** — i.e., they stop critiquing. Exp. 4 manipulated transportation *downward* via processing instructions, and belief change dropped with it.
→ **Application:** The single biggest risk to your "before" sequence is anything that makes the viewer *evaluate* instead of *inhabit*. That means: no on-screen bullet lists during the pain sequence, no VO narrator explaining what we're seeing, no logo. The moment a brand mark appears you have issued a "processing instruction." Hold the 마인드스코프 logo until after the turn.

**2. Braddock & Dillard (2016).** *Meta-analytic evidence for the persuasive effect of narratives...* Communication Monographs, 83(4), 446–467. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03637751.2015.1128555
Narrative exposure → beliefs r=.17 (k=37), attitudes r=.19 (k=40), intentions r=.17 (k=28), behaviors r=.23 (k=5). **Medium of presentation did not moderate** the effect.
→ **Application:** This is the license for the whole approach — but note the effects are modest (r≈.2). A story buys you a real but bounded lift; it does not replace the product demo. Budget roughly: story 60%, mechanism 40%.

**3. Van Laer, de Ruyter, Visconti & Wetzels (2014).** *The extended transportation-imagery model.* Journal of Consumer Research, 40(5), 797–817. DOI 10.1086/673383. https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/40/5/797/2907487
132 effect sizes from 76 articles; effects range ~.10–.50 and survive file-drawer tests. Antecedents split into **story characteristics** (identifiable character, imageable plot, verisimilitude) and **receiver characteristics** (familiarity, attention, transportability, demographic similarity).
→ **Application:** "Demographic similarity" is the cheapest lever you have and most Korean SaaS videos waste it. The protagonist should be a 30s–40s 여성 상담사 in a real 상담실 — not a model, not a 대표원장. Your buyer is often the 센터장, but your *emotional* protagonist should be the person the 센터장 feels guilty about.

**4. Green (2004).** *Transportation into narrative worlds: prior knowledge and perceived realism.* Discourse Processes, 38(2), 247–266. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15326950dp3802_5
N=152. Transportation correlated positively with perceived realism, and **participants with relevant prior experience were more transported.**
→ **Application:** Verisimilitude details are not decoration, they are the mechanism. Get the props right: 실제 상담일지 양식, 초기접수면접지, K-WISC-V 프로파일 용지, 바우처 청구 화면, 형광펜 자국이 있는 회기 기록. One wrong form on screen and your only high-prior-knowledge audience (센터장/상담사) drops out of the story.

**5. Deighton, Romer & McQueen (1989).** *Using drama to persuade.* JCR, 16(3), 335–343. https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/16/3/335/1818805
40 TV commercials rated on a dramatization scale, shown to 1,215 people. **Arguments are processed evaluatively; dramas are processed empathically** — the same measures load differently by form.
→ **Application:** Hard structural rule: the "before" half must be *drama* (no claims, no numbers, no VO), the "after" half may be *argument* (features, ROI, 도입 절차). Mixing forms in the pain sequence converts empathic processing into evaluative processing and kills it.

**6. Slater & Rouner (2002).** *Entertainment—education and elaboration likelihood.* Communication Theory, 12(2), 173–191. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2885.2002.tb00265.x
E-ELM: absorption in narrative and response to characters **suppress counterarguing** of implicitly persuasive content.
→ **Application:** Your viewer's default counterargument is "우리 센터는 그렇게까지 비효율적이지 않은데." A story where a specific counselor is visibly still at the center at 9:40 PM is much harder to counterargue than the claim "상담센터는 행정업무에 시간을 낭비합니다."

**7. Cohen (2001).** *Defining identification.* Mass Communication and Society, 4(3), 245–264. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/S15327825MCS0403_01
Identification is distinct from liking or parasocial interaction: it is temporarily **adopting the character's perspective and goals** — losing self-awareness.
→ **Application:** Shoot the pain sequence in tight POV/over-shoulder — the counselor's hands on the keyboard, her view of the screen, her view of the clock — not in observational wide shots. Wide shots produce sympathy (watching her); POV produces identification (being her).

**8. Friestad & Wright (1994).** *The persuasion knowledge model.* JCR, 21(1), 1–31.
Once persuasion knowledge activates, consumers "detach" and discount the message.
→ **Application:** Concretely: no upbeat corporate music under the pain scene, no obviously staged sighing, no actor looking at camera. Over-performed suffering is the single most common trigger of persuasion-knowledge activation in Korean B2B video. Underplay it — she doesn't sigh dramatically, she just quietly re-types the same 이름 for the fourth time.

## 2. 한 사람의 고통이 전이되게 만들기 — Identifiability, Contagion, Empathy Gaps

**9. Small, Loewenstein & Slovic (2007).** *Sympathy and callousness.* OBHDP, 102(2), 143–153. https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/SympathyCallous.pdf
People give more to an identified victim than to statistical victims — and **priming deliberative/analytic thinking reduces giving to the identified victim without raising giving to statistical victims.** ⚠️ Note a 2023 replication+extension (Maier et al., *Collabra*) revisits this: https://mgto.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Maier-etal-2023-Collabra-Small-etal-2007-replication-extension-print.pdf
→ **Application:** Never open with a market statistic. "전국 상담센터의 78%가…" is a deliberation prime that measurably suppresses the feeling you're trying to create. Open on 김선영 상담사, 화요일 밤 9시 40분.

**10. Kogut & Ritov (2005).** *The "identified victim" effect: an identified group, or just a single individual?* Journal of Behavioral Decision Making, 18(3), 157–167. https://pluto.huji.ac.il/~msiritov/KogutRitovIdentified.pdf
**Singularity effect:** identification only works for a *single* victim. Identifying each member of a group of eight had essentially no effect.
→ **Application:** One counselor. Not "a team of three." A montage of several overworked counselors *destroys* the effect this research predicts — it converts a person into a category.

**11. Lee & Feeley (2016).** *The identifiable victim effect: a meta-analytic review.* Social Influence, 11(3), 199–215. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15534510.2016.1216891
41 studies; overall effect real but **modest (r=.05)**. Moderators that strengthen it: victim is a **child**, a **photograph** is shown, and the victim is **not responsible** for their plight.
→ **Application:** Two things. (a) You are in the one industry where "child" is native — the cost of the counselor's admin hours is *time not spent with a child*. Show the child waiting, or the parent's unanswered 문자. (b) Non-responsibility matters: never imply the center is disorganized. The pain must be framed as imposed by the system (바우처 청구 양식, 보고서 서식, 결제/영수증 처리), not by her incompetence. This is also the reactance fix in §4.

**12. Slovic (2007).** *"If I look at the mass I will never act": psychic numbing and genocide.* Judgment and Decision Making, 2(2), 79–95. https://www.sas.upenn.edu/~baron/journal/7303a/jdm7303a.htm
Statistics are "human beings with the tears dried off" — affect does not scale with numbers; scope insensitivity is the default.
→ **Application:** If you must use "월 40시간 절감," attach it to *her*: "선영 씨는 이번 달에 40시간을 돌려받았습니다" — and then show what she did with the 40 hours. A number alone is a numbing device.

**13. Hatfield, Cacioppo & Rapson (1993).** *Emotional contagion.* Current Directions in Psychological Science, 2(3), 96–99. https://www.elainehatfield.com/uploads/3/2/2/5/3225640/50._hatfield_cacioppo__rapson_1993.pdf
Primitive emotional contagion: automatic mimicry and synchronization of facial expression, vocalization, posture → convergent emotion. Facial feedback matters (Botox studies impair recognition).
→ **Application:** You need **a clean, held, frontal close-up of her face** at the peak — minimum 2.5–3 seconds, no cut. Fast MTV-style cutting during the pain sequence prevents mimicry from ever engaging. Also: micro-expressions transfer, performed sadness does not. Direct the actor toward "blank, depleted" rather than "sad."

**14. Lamm, Decety & Singer (2011).** *Meta-analytic evidence for common and distinct neural networks...* NeuroImage, 54(3), 2492–2502. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20946964/
Image-based meta-analysis of 9 fMRI studies + coordinate-based of 32: empathy for pain recruits bilateral anterior insula and medial/anterior cingulate — **overlapping with directly experienced pain.**
→ **Application:** Vicarious affect is real and neural, but it needs a *target*. Keep the object of pain visible in frame with her — the stack of 서류, the blinking cursor, the clock. Insula-level responses in the literature are driven by observable cues of distress, not by inferred abstract hardship.

**15. Loewenstein (2005).** *Hot–cold empathy gaps and medical decision making.* Health Psychology, 24(4S), S49–S56. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16045419/
In a cold (calm) state people systematically fail to appreciate how a hot state feels — including **interpersonally**, between two people in different affective states.
→ **Application:** This is *the* diagnosis of your problem. Your viewer watches your video in a cold state at 2pm. You cannot describe the 9:40pm state; you must induce a compressed version of it. Which leads directly to #18 (fluency) — make the viewer *experience* friction, don't narrate it.

**16. Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber (1989).** *The curse of knowledge in economic settings.* Journal of Political Economy, 97(5), 1232–1254. https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/CurseknowledgeEconSet.pdf
Better-informed agents cannot ignore their private information when predicting less-informed judgments. Market discipline reduces the curse by ~50% but does not eliminate it.
+ **Newton (1990),** Stanford dissertation, *Overconfidence in the communication of intent: heard and unheard melodies* — tappers predicted listeners would identify 50% of tunes; actual **2.5% of 120**.
→ **Application:** Your own team is the tapper. Anyone at 마인드스코프 who says "그건 다 아는 내용이잴" is exhibiting exactly this. **Test the rough cut on someone who has never worked in a 상담센터** and ask them to describe what went wrong in the before scene. If they can't name the specific failure, the pain didn't land — regardless of what your team thinks. (Heath & Heath's *Made to Stick* popularized these two studies; the underlying research is Camerer et al. 1989 + Newton 1990, not the book.)

**17. Taylor & Thompson (1982).** *Stalking the elusive "vividness" effect.* Psychological Review, 89(2), 155–181. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1982-11205-001
A useful corrective: reviewing memorability, imageability, and affective-impact accounts, they conclude vividness effects are **weak and inconsistent in the lab**, and appear mainly under **differential attention** — conditions resembling real-world informational competition.
→ **Application:** Vividness alone is not magic. It works when the vivid item *out-competes* alternatives for attention. So: one vivid detail, held long, in silence — not a dense montage of vivid details. Cut the montage down to the single most specific object (e.g., the 손으로 옮겨 적는 검사 원점수).

**18. Trope & Liberman (2010).** *Construal-level theory of psychological distance.* Psychological Review, 117(2), 440–463. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2010-14834-012 *(record incl. correction notice)*
Greater psychological distance (time, space, social, hypotheticality) → more abstract construal; near distance → concrete.
→ **Application:** Language rule for the script. Abstract = distant = unfelt. Ban "업무 효율화," "행정 부담," "생산성." Replace with near/concrete: "같은 이름을 네 번 옮겨 적습니다." "화요일 밤 9시 40분." Concrete language *is* the distance-closing device.

## 3. Before → After 구조 설계

**19. Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber & Redelmeier (1993).** *When more pain is preferred to less: adding a better end.* Psychological Science, 4(6), 401–405. **+ Redelmeier & Kahneman (1996),** *Patients' memories of painful medical treatments,* Pain, 66(1), 3–8 (colonoscopy n=154, lithotripsy n=133). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0304395996029946
Retrospective evaluation ≈ average of the **peak** and the **end**; total duration is largely neglected.
→ **Application (the single most actionable finding here):**
- **Do not make the pain sequence long.** Duration buys you nothing in memory. Make it *sharp*. 25–35 seconds is enough if the peak is severe.
- **Place the worst moment as a single identifiable peak,** not spread evenly. One shot must be clearly the worst: e.g., she notices at 9:40pm that a number was mistyped, and starts the 보고서 over.
- **The final 5 seconds carry disproportionate weight.** Do not end on a feature list or a pricing card. End on her face — leaving the center while it's still light outside, phone dark, no notification.

**20. Loewenstein & Prelec (1993).** *Preferences for sequences of outcomes.* Psychological Review, 100(1), 91–108. https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PrefSeqOutcomes.pdf
When outcomes are explicitly framed *as a sequence*, people prefer **improving** sequences and will postpone the better outcome to the end.
→ **Application:** Empirical support for bad→good ordering, and for making the sequence *explicit*. A visible time/date stamp across the film (화요일 21:40 → 화요일 18:10) makes the improvement legible as a sequence rather than as two unrelated scenes. Never open with the happy after-state.

**21. Nabi (2015).** *Emotional flow in persuasive health messages.* Health Communication, 30(2), 114–124. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25470436/
Argues the *evolution* of emotion across a message — the flow — matters more than any single emotional state, and that emotional shifts promote elaboration.
→ **Application:** Name your emotional arc explicitly in the edit plan and make the **transition moment** a designed beat, not a dissolve. Something like: 피로 → 체념 → (전환) → 의외/안도 → 회복. Give the shift one held second of silence — the audio drop is the shift.

**22. Reber, Winkielman & Schwarz (1998),** *Effects of perceptual fluency on affective judgments,* Psychological Science, 9(1), 45–48 (https://pages.ucsd.edu/~pwinkiel/reber-winkielman-schwarz-Fluency-PS-1998.pdf) **+ Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman (2004),** *Processing fluency and aesthetic pleasure,* PSPR, 8(4), 364–382.
Easier-to-process stimuli are liked more; fluency is experienced as affectively positive and misattributed to the object.
→ **Application:** Design the two halves as opposite fluency experiences. **Before:** cluttered frame, low figure–ground contrast, cool fluorescent color, overlapping UI windows, layered audio (전화벨 + 키보드 + 알림음). **After:** high contrast, single focal element, one clean sound. The viewer will misattribute their own processing ease to 마인드스코프.

**23. Song & Schwarz (2008).** *If it's hard to read, it's hard to do.* Psychological Science, 19(10), 986–988. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19000208/
Difficult-to-read fonts made people estimate **83% more time** for an exercise routine (Study 1) and **59% more** for a recipe (Study 2), and reduced willingness to do it.
→ **Application:** The most direct answer to the hot–cold empathy gap (#15). Render the "before" **on-screen text/forms in a genuinely hard-to-read way** — small, dense, low-contrast, cramped Korean 서식 with 8pt 명조 — for 2–3 seconds. The viewer physically experiences effort. Then the "after" UI in large, airy 고딕. You have transferred effort, not described it.

**24. Herr, Sherman & Fazio (1983).** *On the consequences of priming: assimilation and contrast effects.* JESP, 19(3), 323–340. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0022103183900264
**Extreme** context stimuli produce contrast; moderate ones produce assimilation.
→ **Application:** Justifies making the "before" clearly extreme rather than merely annoying — an extreme anchor makes the "after" evaluate better by contrast. But #26–#28 cap how far you can push. The resolution: extreme in *volume and duration of work*, moderate in *emotional performance*. Extreme workload, understated acting.

**25. Loewenstein (1994).** *The psychology of curiosity: a review and reinterpretation.* Psychological Bulletin, 116(1), 75–98.
Information-gap theory: curiosity is cognitively induced deprivation from a perceived gap; it **peaks at intermediate knowledge** and falls when you know almost nothing or almost everything.
→ **Application:** Open the loop with a partial, not a total, mystery. A cold open of her writing something onto a sticky note that we can't read, or the timestamp 21:40 on screen with no explanation, creates a gap. A cryptic abstract opener ("변화가 시작됩니다") creates *no* gap because there's no partial knowledge to complete. Close the loop explicitly at the turn.

**26. ⚠️ Zeigarnik effect — largely unsupported.** Meta-analysis: *Interruption, recall and resumption: a meta-analysis of the Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina effects,* Humanities & Social Sciences Communications (2025). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w
59 studies; pooled recall ratio **0.99** — essentially **no memory advantage for interrupted tasks**. The **Ovsiankina effect (tendency to resume)** holds better.
→ **Application:** Do not build the video's structure on "open loops improve memory" — that claim does not survive meta-analysis. Use Loewenstein's information gap (#25) as your justification for suspense instead, and keep the loop short. This is one to correct if it's in your creative brief.

## 4. 얼마나 세게 밀어붙일 것인가 — Framing, Fear, Reactance

**27. Kahneman & Tversky (1979).** *Prospect theory.* Econometrica, 47(2), 263–291.
Value is defined over gains/losses **relative to a reference point**; the loss function is steeper than the gain function.
→ **Application:** The lever isn't "loss-framed copy," it's **reference-point setting**. The before-sequence *is* the reference point. Once the viewer has adopted 18:10 퇴근 as the reference, staying at 21:40 becomes a loss they are currently absorbing every day.

**28. O'Keefe & Jensen (2007, 2009).** Prevention meta-analysis: 93 studies, N=21,656, gain-framed advantage **r≈.03**. Detection meta-analysis: 53 studies, N=9,145, loss-framed advantage **r≈−.04**. J. of Health Communication / Journal of Communication. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1460-2466.2009.01417.x
Framing differences are real but **tiny**, and largely driven by specific behavior sub-types.
→ **Application:** Stop optimizing gain-vs-loss headline wording; the meta-analytic payoff is near zero. Spend that effort on transportation and concreteness (#3, #18, #23), where effects are an order of magnitude larger. Practically: "매일 3시간을 돌려드립니다" vs "매일 3시간을 잃고 계십니다" is not worth an argument.

**29. Tannenbaum, Hepler, Zimmerman, Saul, Jacobs, Wilson & Albarracín (2015).** *Appealing to fear: a meta-analysis.* Psychological Bulletin, 141(6), 1178–1204. https://socialactionlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Tannenbaum_Appealing-to-Fear-A-Meta-Analysis-of-Fear-Appeal-Effectiveness-and-Theories_2015.pdf
127 papers, 248 samples, N=27,372. Composite **d = 0.27**. Effectiveness *increases* with higher depicted fear, and with **efficacy statements** plus high susceptibility/severity. They report essentially **no identified conditions under which fear appeals backfire**.

**30. Witte & Allen (2000).** *A meta-analysis of fear appeals.* Health Education & Behavior, 27(5), 591–615. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/109019810002700506
EPPM: **strong fear + high efficacy → greatest behavior change; strong fear + low efficacy → greatest defensive avoidance/reactance.**
→ **Application (combining #29 and #30):** Agitate hard — the evidence does not support timidity — **but the efficacy content must be adjacent, not deferred.** Concretely: do not put the solution reveal at 0:70 of a 0:90 video. Put the turn at roughly the 40% mark, and make the solution look *easy to adopt* (response efficacy: "이 작업이 사라집니다") **and easy for her specifically** (self-efficacy: "기존 서식 그대로, 교육 없이"). Self-efficacy is the piece Korean SaaS videos routinely omit, and it's the one EPPM says prevents the defensive response.

**31. Dillard & Shen (2005).** *On the nature of reactance...* Communication Monographs, 72(2), 144–168. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03637750500111815
Two studies (N=196, N=200): reactance is best measured as a **composite of anger + negative cognitions**, which fully mediated the effect of freedom-threatening language on attitude and intention.
→ **Application:** Reactance here is triggered less by fear than by **implied blame and imperative language**. Audit the script for: 2nd-person accusation ("당신은 아직도 수기로…"), imperatives ("지금 바꾸세요"), and any implication the center is behind the times. Rewrite in 3rd-person observation about 선영 씨. A 센터장 who feels judged generates anger, and anger fully mediates the failure.

**32. Allen (1991).** *Meta-analysis comparing the persuasiveness of one-sided and two-sided messages.* Western Journal of Speech Communication, 55. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ437341
**Two-sided *with refutation* > one-sided > two-sided without refutation.** Consistent with the discounting hypothesis. (Note a published cautionary re-analysis: O'Keefe, ERIC EJ462224.)
→ **Application:** Name the objection and refute it — don't just mention it. The real objection in this market: "선생님들이 새 시스템 못 쓰실 텐데요" / "기존 기록은 어떻게 하나요." Show a 60대 상담사 using it in one shot, or show the 데이터 이관 in three seconds. A two-sided message that raises the concern without answering it is *worse than saying nothing*.

**33. Zebregs, van den Putte, Neijens & de Graaf (2015).** *The differential impact of statistical and narrative evidence on beliefs, attitude, and intention: a meta-analysis.* Health Communication, 30(3), 282–289. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24836931/
15 studies: **statistical evidence stronger on beliefs and attitude; narrative evidence stronger on intention.**
→ **Application:** This settles the "story vs. numbers" debate for you — you need both, in the right slots. Story does the emotional work and drives 문의/도입 의향; **numbers must appear in the after-half to make the belief ("이게 실제로 시간을 줄여준다") stick.** Put one hard statistic on screen after the turn, never before it.

**34. ⚠️ Gourville (2006).** *Eager sellers and stony buyers.* **Harvard Business Review** — practitioner article, not peer-reviewed; builds on Kahneman & Tversky's loss aversion and the endowment effect. Argues a **9x mismatch** (3× seller overvaluation × 3× buyer endowment) and that gains must outweigh losses ~3:1 before adoption.
→ **Application:** Treat the ratio as a heuristic, not evidence. The usable implication is real though: the counselor is endowed with her current 엑셀/한글 양식 and her workarounds. Show explicitly that **she keeps what she's endowed with** — her forms, her wording, her records — and only loses the labor. "쓰던 서식 그대로."

## 5. 한국 상담센터 맥락에 특화된 근거

**35. Han & Shavitt (1994).** *Persuasion and culture: advertising appeals in individualistic and collectivistic societies.* Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 30(4), 326–350. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002210318471016X/pdf
Study 1 (content analysis US vs Korea) and Study 2 (controlled experiment in both countries): **in Korea, appeals emphasizing ingroup benefits, harmony and family integrity were more persuasive; individual-benefit appeals were less persuasive than in the US.**
→ **Application:** The payoff line should not be "당신의 시간을 아껴드립니다" (individual benefit — the US frame). It should be ingroup-framed: **"선생님이 아이에게 돌아갈 시간"** or "센터 전체가 같은 기록을 봅니다." The emotional resolution shot should show her with a colleague or a child/parent, not alone enjoying free time. This is the most Korea-specific and most commonly violated finding in this report.

**36. Lim and colleagues on Korean school counselors** — *Impact of state and trait emotion regulation on daily emotional exhaustion among Korean school counsellors,* Stress and Health (2024), https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/smi.3452 ; *Effects of surface acting on exhaustion of Korean school counselors* and *The reciprocal relationship between emotion regulation and emotional exhaustion among Korean school counselors,* Journal of Counseling & Development (2025), https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jcad.12552
Korean counselors carry counseling plus **non-counseling administrative duties**; role conflict and role ambiguity from 비상담 업무 are documented burnout drivers, and **a supportive administrative climate negatively predicted exhaustion.**
→ **Application:** This gives you a defensible, citable framing that is *not* "your center is inefficient": the problem is **역할 갈등** — she trained to do counseling and spends her evenings doing administration. That framing is (a) empirically supported in a Korean sample, (b) blame-free (satisfies #11 non-responsibility and #31 reactance), and (c) emotionally exact. Consider a single restrained line: "상담을 하려고 이 일을 시작했는데." ⚠️ These are school-counselor samples, not private 상담센터/발달센터 — don't cite them as a claim about your exact market.

## 6. 종합 — 90초 편집 설계 (what the evidence jointly implies)

**Three rules the research most strongly supports, if you keep only three:**
1. **One named person, never a montage of many** (#10 singularity, #9, #12).

## 7. 검증하지 못한 것 / 주의사항 (flagged honestly)

- **Van Laer et al. (2014) per-antecedent effect sizes** — I could not extract the moderator table (the open-access PDF failed to parse). I verified only: 132 effect sizes / 76 articles, range ~.10–.50, robust to file-drawer. **Do not cite specific per-antecedent numbers from me.** The 2018 follow-up (Van Laer, Feiereisen & Visconti, *Journal of Business Research*, 96, 135–146) reports moderators including commercial vs. non-commercial domain, user- vs. professionally-generated, and solo vs. group reception — that one I saw only in summary form too.
- **Allen (1991) page numbers** unverified (record confirms Western Journal of Speech Communication, vol. 55, 1991). A published cautionary re-analysis exists (O'Keefe, *Western Journal of Communication*, 1993, ERIC EJ462224) — the "two-sided refutational wins" conclusion is contested in magnitude, not direction.
- **Small et al. (2007)** has a 2023 replication/extension (Maier et al., *Collabra: Psychology*) that revisits the effect. **Lee & Feeley's meta-analytic r = .05 is small.** The identifiable victim effect is directionally well-supported but modest — do not oversell it internally.
- **Zeigarnik effect: actively unsupported** by the 2025 meta-analysis (59 studies, recall ratio 0.99). Flagged as #26. If this is in the creative brief, remove it.
- **Gourville (2006) "9x effect"** is HBR, **not peer-reviewed**, and the 3:1 / 9x figures are illustrative arithmetic, not a measured meta-analytic parameter.
- **"Problem-Agitate-Solve" (PAS): I found no peer-reviewed research under that name.** It is copywriting folklore. The closest defensible evidence base is the combination of Deighton et al. (1989) drama-vs-argument, Nabi (2015) emotional flow, Allen (1991) sidedness, and the fear-appeal meta-analyses (Tannenbaum 2015 / Witte & Allen 2000) on how far agitation can go. I'd recommend not citing "PAS is proven" to a client.
- **Heath & Heath's *Made to Stick*** is a trade book. Its curse-of-knowledge chapter rests on Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber (1989) and Newton (1990) — cite those.
- **Lim et al. Korean counselor studies** are **school**-counselor samples. Your market is private 상담센터 / 아동발달센터. Directionally supportive, not a market statistic. I found no peer-reviewed study quantifying administrative hours in Korean private counseling centers — if you need that number, it likely has to come from your own customer data.
- All findings above were verified via publisher/PubMed/repository records and published abstracts. I did not read full texts, so I have not independently checked methods or the analyses behind reported effect sizes.
